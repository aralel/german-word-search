Privacy Policy

For the German Word Search app on iOS, iPadOS and Android.
Last updated September 17, 2026 · Published by Aralel GmbH

German Word Search is a word puzzle game for learning German noun genders. It has no accounts, no sign-in and no social features, and it never asks you for your name, your email address or your location. This page explains the data that is involved, which is mostly advertising and purchase data handled by Apple, Google and our subscription provider.

The short version. We don't collect anything that identifies you personally. Ads may use an advertising identifier, but only if you allow it. Purchases are handled by the app store. Diagnostics and usage analytics can be switched off in Settings → Data collection inside the app, and your game progress never leaves your device.

2. What the app never collects

There is no account system, so none of the following is requested, stored or transmitted: your name, email address, phone number or postal address; your location; your contacts, photos, calendar, microphone or camera; your health or fitness data; your browsing or search history outside the app; or any payment card details.

3. Advertising and your consent

The free version shows ads supplied by Google AdMob: a banner, and an optional short video you can choose to watch in exchange for a hint. Premium subscribers see no banner.

Whether those ads are personalised depends entirely on your answer to a consent prompt:

  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the app shows a Google-certified consent message the first time it runs. Decline, and ads continue but are non-personalised.
  • On iOS, personalised ads additionally require the system's App Tracking Transparency permission. If you decline that prompt — or never see it — the advertising identifier is not available to us and ads stay non-personalised.
  • You can change your mind at any time: on iOS in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, and in the app under Settings → Privacy options where your region requires it.

Google also uses Apple's SKAdNetwork on iOS, which reports install conversions to advertisers in a form Apple designs to be non-identifying. Google's handling of ad data is governed by its own policy, linked in section 10.

4. Purchases and subscriptions

Premium unlocks the B1, B2, C1 and C2 word levels. Payment is taken by Apple or Google, never by us — we never see your card number, billing address or any other payment detail.

We use RevenueCat to check whether a subscription is active. RevenueCat receives the purchase receipt and an anonymous identifier it generates for your installation, which is how the app knows to keep Premium unlocked and how "Restore purchases" works. It does not receive your name or payment details.

5. Diagnostics and usage analytics

The app has a single Data collection switch in Settings that controls all of it. It is on by default, and turning it off stops every item below at the source.

  • Usage events. Short messages recording that something happened — for example that the translation language changed — together with the device model and app version. Sent only if a reporting endpoint is configured for the build you installed.
  • Crash and performance reports (Android only). Firebase Crashlytics, Firebase Performance Monitoring and Sentry record crashes, error traces and timing data. Switching data collection off disables Crashlytics collection and shuts the Sentry SDK down entirely.
  • Wrong-answer reports. If you tap "report wrong answer" in a quiz, the word, its translation and the article the app claimed are sent to us so we can correct the word list. Nothing is sent unless you confirm the report.

6. Translation, speech and your game data

Word meanings are translated and read aloud on your device. On iOS this uses Apple's Translation framework and speech synthesiser; on Android it uses Google ML Kit's on-device translation, which downloads a language model the first time you use a language. The words you look up are not sent to us.

Your game progress — the puzzle in play, scores and statistics, your vocabulary notebook, your streak and your settings — is stored only on your device, in the operating system's standard app storage. We have no copy of it and no way to read it. Deleting the app deletes it.

7. Leaderboards and achievements

If you are signed in to Game Center (iOS) or Google Play Games (Android), your score and achievements are submitted to that service under the identity you hold with Apple or Google, and their privacy policies govern it. Signing in is optional; declining costs you nothing but the leaderboard. The in-app "cross-store leaderboard" screen currently keeps your entry on your own device only.

8. Children

The app is intended for a general audience of German learners aged 13 and over, and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because there are no accounts, we have no way to identify a user's age; if you believe a child has provided information to us through the app, contact us and we will delete it.

9. Your rights and choices

Whatever your jurisdiction, these controls are in your hands:

  • Stop analytics and diagnostics: Settings → Data collection → off.
  • Stop personalised ads: decline the consent prompt, or withdraw consent in Settings → Privacy options; on iOS also via Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Erase everything: delete the app. All game data is local, so removal is complete.
  • Remove leaderboard entries: through your Game Center or Play Games account settings.

If the GDPR or UK GDPR applies to you, you have the right to access, correct, erase, restrict or object to the processing of your personal data, and to data portability — write to us at the address below and we will respond within one month. Our lawful bases are your consent (for personalised advertising) and our legitimate interest in keeping the app stable and functional (for diagnostics, which you can switch off). You may also complain to your local supervisory authority. If you are a California resident, we do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined by the CCPA/CPRA.

10. Third parties we rely on
